AI-Assisted Triage for Customer Inquiries

Can an AI agent get routine emails to the right team faster while sending unclear cases to a person?

The industry problem

Customers email companies about bills, subscriptions, orders, and technical issues. When those messages land in a shared inbox, someone has to read each one and decide who should handle it. A wrong handoff adds rework and delays the response.

McKinsey's 2026 customer care survey describes support teams facing limited budgets and rising customer expectations. It also describes a shift toward always-on service, with AI helping handle routine tasks and people handling more complex cases.

AI could change the workload in both directions. Customers may send more inquiries if AI makes them easier to draft and follow up on, while better self-service could resolve some issues before an email is sent. Our proof of concept tests whether automated triage can maintain fast, accurate handoffs as volume rises.

How we measure success

The evaluation considers both classification quality and the operational risk of sending an inquiry to the wrong team. Correctly identifying cases that need human review is particularly important: not every email should be automatically routed.

Metric What it tells us
Accuracy Share of all emails assigned the expected destination.
Macro F1 Balanced classification performance across all destination categories.
Per-class precision and recall How reliably each destination is selected and how many relevant cases it captures.
Auto-route rate Share of emails assigned to a specialist team rather than review.
Incorrect auto-route rate Share of all emails incorrectly sent to a specialist team. This is a key measure of routing risk.
Review recall Share of cases expected to require human review that are correctly assigned to review.
Latency Time taken by the classifier to produce a routing decision.
Consistency Whether repeated classifications of the same email produce the same destination.

The evaluation also examines performance by language, complexity, email length, customer tone and security-related test cases.

Business case

The potential business value comes from reducing manual sorting, avoiding incorrect handoffs and allowing support teams to focus their attention on cases that require human judgement.

Potential value drivers

  • Less time spent manually reading and categorising incoming emails.
  • Fewer incorrect transfers between support teams.
  • Faster assignment of routine customer inquiries.
  • More consistent handling of ambiguous or sensitive cases.
  • Scalable processing as inquiry volumes change.

What needs to be measured

A quantified business case requires operational data such as email volumes, current handling time, labour cost, error rates, average resolution time and the cost of implementing and operating the solution.

Current status: These are potential value drivers, not yet measured savings. The proof of concept currently evaluates classification performance rather than realised financial benefits.

Agentic AI proof of concept

The proof of concept investigates an AI-assisted workflow that classifies incoming customer emails and assigns them to an appropriate destination. Unclear, out-of-scope or multi-topic inquiries can be sent to human review rather than automatically routed.

Routing destinations

Destination Typical inquiry
Billing Invoices, payments, disputed charges, refunds and credits.
Orders Physical orders, delivery, tracking, missing or damaged items and returns.
Technical Product or software faults, crashes, login and connectivity issues.
Review Ambiguous, out-of-scope, sensitive or cross-department cases requiring human judgement.

Current technical setup

  • Local inference through Ollama.
  • Qwen3 0.6B language model.
  • Structured output validation using Pydantic.
  • Fallback to human review when the classifier output is invalid.
  • Docker-based development and evaluation environment.
  • MLflow experiment tracking for evaluation results.

Evaluation approach

The original classifier (P0) is retained as the reference implementation. Experimental prompt changes are evaluated separately through P1, using the same expanded dataset and evaluation framework.

The evaluation script measures classifier predictions. It does not by itself establish successful end-to-end email delivery, operational handling or business impact.

Evaluation dataset: 800 synthetic emails

An expanded dataset of 800 synthetic customer emails was created to evaluate classification quality across multiple languages, inquiry types and levels of complexity.

Dimension Coverage
Total records 800
Routing categories Billing, Orders, Technical and Review (200 each)
Languages English, German, French and Italian (200 each)
Complexity 400 straightforward, 240 ambiguous, 160 multi-intent
Email length 720 short and 80 long
Customer tone Neutral, frustrated, urgent and threatening (200 each)
Security-related cases 40 cases (5% of the dataset)

The dataset includes expected routing labels and supporting metadata such as language, complexity, tone, issue type and classification rationale.

Performance so far

The original P0 classifier is the reference point. Two P1 prompt experiments have been evaluated against the expanded 800-email dataset. Both produced lower classification performance than P0.

Experiment comparison

Metric P0 reference P1 experiment 1 P1 experiment 2
Accuracy 81.75% 51.6% 46.0%
Macro F1 0.817 0.467 0.411
Auto-route rate 78.0% 77.1% 93.6%
Incorrect auto-route rate 15.38% 43.6% 53.4%
Review recall 76.5% 72.5% 23.0%
Security test success 50.0% 37.5% 37.5%

Results are from the current evaluation runs. The P1 columns represent separate prompt experiments, not successive improvements. The P0 reference is retained unchanged.

What the experiments tell us

  • The first P1 experiment reduced accuracy by 30.15 percentage points compared with P0.
  • The second P1 experiment reduced accuracy by 35.75 percentage points compared with P0.
  • The second P1 experiment increased automatic routing to 93.6%, but review recall fell to 23.0%. This indicates that too many cases were being automatically assigned instead of being sent for human review.
  • Neither experiment improved the overall balance between classification quality and safe routing.
Current decision: P0 remains the champion reference. The experimental P1 prompts are not accepted as replacements.

Next experiment: Billing versus Orders

The next prompt refinement focuses on distinguishing financial issues from physical order and delivery issues. In particular, return requests should be classified as Orders unless the main complaint concerns a missing refund or disputed charge.

This refinement is a hypothesis to test, not a demonstrated improvement. Its results should be added here only after the evaluation has completed.

Evaluation limitations

  • The dataset is synthetic and cannot fully represent real customer language or operational conditions.
  • Security-related test results are preliminary and should not be interpreted as conclusive evidence of prompt-injection resistance.
  • Latency measures classifier execution, not end-to-end workflow processing.
  • Classification results do not establish actual email routing success or realised business savings.
